The problem we were funded to solve

Voice AI that sounds human is no longer the hard part. Affording it is. The good models are metered by the minute and priced for contact centres, so the bill grows with every call a business takes — which is precisely the wrong shape for a four-person practice, a builder or a boarding kennels. The project takes the cost floor out: the same quality of conversation, at a price a small business can put on a monthly invoice without thinking about it.

  1. 01

    Foundation models, adapted rather than rebuilt

    We start from open foundation models and adapt them to the job, so the effort goes into the last mile — accents, trade vocabulary, interruptions, people changing their mind mid-sentence — instead of re-proving what the field already knows.

  2. 02

    Speech data that sounds like the actual callers

    Purpose-built training sets for both directions: text-to-speech that doesn't read like an American call centre, and speech-to-text that copes with regional accents, background noise and the words a trade customer actually uses on the phone.

  3. 03

    Open-source LLMs on infrastructure we control

    The reasoning layer runs on models we host. The cost per conversation doesn't move when a vendor reprices its API, and a caller's details don't have to leave the estate to be understood.

  4. 04

    Network accelerators, because latency is the tell

    Answer within a beat and a caller talks to it normally. Answer a second late and they know. Closing that gap is inference acceleration and the network path around it — not just picking a faster model.

That is the difference between voice AI being something an enterprise buys and something a plumber can.

Is Sonti right for your agency?

We work best with agencies who own the client relationship and want a production team behind it, not a marketplace of freelancers to manage.

A good fit if…

  • You've got live client requests for AI, chatbots, voice or automation you can't fulfil in-house
  • You want to sell AI services under your brand without hiring engineers
  • You're after recurring monthly fees — these builds need hosting, tuning and support, so the client pays every month instead of once
  • You'd rather have one accountable UK contact than manage developers yourself

  • You want to hire a single freelancer by the hour and manage them yourself

Questions agencies ask first

The ones that come up on nearly every first call. Anything else, ask us on the call itself.

Is everything white-label?

Yes, by default. Scopes, decks, code, documentation and the finished product carry your brand. If you'd like us to join client calls, we come as your technical team. If you'd rather your client never hears the name Sonti, they won't.

Who owns the code and the IP?

You or your client, as set out in the contract — never us. Everything is handed over with source, documentation and admin access, so there's no lock-in to Sonti or to any single AI vendor.

Where does the data go, and is it GDPR-compliant?

You contract with our UK business, with a data processing agreement as standard. Builds are hosted in UK or EU regions unless your client specifies otherwise. Our engineers in India work under NDA with role-based access, and we can restrict production data to the UK team where a client requires it.

How quickly can something be live?

A scope and an estimate in hours within two working days of the brief. As a rule of thumb, a chatbot on a client's existing content is live in three to four weeks, a voice agent in four to eight, and a single automation in days. Your estimate gives the real number for your project.

Can my client talk to the developers?

Your UK analyst is the front door and joins every client conversation. When a technical question needs an engineer, one joins the call during the 09:00–14:00 overlap. You never have to translate between your client and a developer.

Which AI models and tools do you use?

Whichever fits the job. We build with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google models as well as open-source ones, and on the platforms your client already pays for — HubSpot, Shopify, Webflow, WordPress, Make, n8n, Zapier and the rest. Each scope names the stack and why.

What happens after launch?

Fixes to the agreed scope are included. Beyond that, monitoring, model updates and small improvements draw from your package hours like any other work, so the thing you sold your client keeps getting better instead of quietly going stale.

How do the hour packages work?

You buy a block of hours — 50, 150 or 400 — and bring us projects. For each one, your UK analyst tells you how many hours it needs before any work starts, and the build draws that from your package. Different projects take different amounts of time; the estimate is what you sign off, not an hourly meter.

What about AI usage costs — tokens and API bills?

Included. The tokens we use to build, test and hand over your project are covered by the package, and because we choose models and tools for efficiency, what we ship is cheap to run as well. There's no separate usage invoice from us.

What if a project needs more hours than I have left?

You'll know before it starts, because every project is estimated up front. If the estimate is bigger than what's left in your package, you buy the next package and the same team carries on — no re-briefing, no new faces.
